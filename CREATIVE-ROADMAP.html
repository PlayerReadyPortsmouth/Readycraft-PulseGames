A place to build, exhibit and celebrate

Creative competitions and a dedicated map-art studio, connected to Survival displays and PulseContent rewards.

Proposed next phase · not implemented or deployed

Where this fits

Complete and review the current SQL and minigame reliability work first. Then inspect the existing Creative world, its permissions, plots and builds before choosing what to retain or move. The old Creative server is outside the current SQL rollout; this proposal does not authorise modifying or restarting it.

Aurora confirmed hand-built map art only, with no image imports. AI screens rendered submissions and flags concerns for human reviewers. No moderation provider or external image upload has been selected or enabled.

Creative competitions

Players nominate their own builds through an in-game menu. Staff set the theme, rules, eligible worlds, submission window and judging criteria.

  • Solo and collaborative entries, with recorded contributors and ownership checks.
  • A visitable gallery of eligible entries and a clear competition status.
  • Judges score against a rubric, leave feedback and declare conflicts.
  • Optional community favourite kept separate from the judged result.
  • Published winners receive unique event trophies, badges or cosmetics.

Map-art studio

A dedicated world of protected plots with tools designed for Java and Bedrock. Players work with blocks and a live preview of the finished map.

  • Palette selection, eyedropper and favourites.
  • Brushes, lines, rectangles, circles and bounded fill.
  • Selections, move/copy, mirror and rotation within owned plots.
  • Shading tools and a later controlled height-based 3D workflow.
  • Persistent drafts, named versions and undo/redo history.

The map-art tool belt

Keep the hotbar consistent: brush · colour palette · eyedropper · shapes · fill · selection · undo · redo · toolbox. The toolbox holds brush size, shading, depth, history, collaboration and submission. Tools are locked to their slots while editing; normal inventories are restored on exit.

Use inventory menus on Java and a Bedrock-friendly form alternative where needed. Every action must respect plot boundaries, collaborator permissions and a per-operation block limit. Large edits run in bounded batches with progress and cancellation. Undo must not revert another collaborator’s later changes silently.

Submission and moderation

Draft → frozen submitted version → automated screening → human review → approved publication → Survival redemption

Every submission receives human review. Automated screening prioritises and explains possible concerns; it does not grant publication. Failed or unavailable screening leaves the entry pending. Reviewers see the exact rendered artwork, title, description, creator and version, with approve, reject or request-changes actions and an audit trail.

Editing a submitted work creates a new draft and invalidates approval for that version. The approved image and content hash remain fixed. Rejection includes an appropriate explanation and a route to resubmit. Pending artwork stays out of public galleries and Survival redemption. Public viewing of unapproved plots needs a deliberate staff policy before release.

Before enabling AI screening, select an approved provider and agree what images are transmitted, retention, reviewer access and safeguarding escalation. Keep player identity out of image requests unless required. A report/revoke action must also cover already approved artwork and redeemed displays.

Redeem in Survival

The player chooses an approved artwork from a shared catalogue and redeems a display copy. PulseContent supplies the item identity, presentation and trophy models; a shared SQL record tracks the approved version, creator, ownership and redemption.

Use a durable redemption reference and retryable delivery record so disconnects cannot grant unlimited copies or silently lose a purchase. Decide whether additional copies are free, limited or cost tokens before enabling an economy. Preserve artist credit. Do not transfer arbitrary Creative inventory into Survival.

Survival placement must respect build protection. Unapproved revisions must never overwrite approved displays. Revocation needs a tested strategy for existing copies and map caches, rather than merely removing the catalogue listing.

Delivery order and acceptance

  1. Creative audit and mockup: inspect the chosen world, plot protection and current plugins; preview entry/judging menus for approval.
  2. Competition pilot: one theme, a small set of nominated builds, two judges, tied result and disconnect tests; award one test trophy.
  3. Map-art prototype: one plot and one map; verify colours, tool safety, history persistence and both clients.
  4. Moderation and redemption: exercise rejected, changed, duplicate, interrupted and revoked submissions; verify Survival display and artist credit.
  5. Expand: murals, collaboration, shading/depth and a broader themed cosmetic catalogue.

Track implementation, local checks, deployment and Aurora’s acceptance separately. No server or plugin is marked ready solely because the design exists.
