# Bots pass 2: Skywars, co-op, Prop Hunt, Block Party, TNT Run, Dodgeball, Quake, hazard games (29 October 2026)

Status: draft PR from `origin/main` 60ea026 (0.8.0). Version not bumped. Not played in a client. Suite: 1123 tests green (1092 before, 31 new).
Read the code only; every "would feel naff" below is a reading of the source, not an observation.

## Diagnosis (before) and what changed

### Skywars (solo, doubles, insane)
- Before: melee fell through to the generic branch in `BotService.control` (walk up, stop at 2.3 blocks, trade hits standing
  still; a miss did nothing). Chests were only opened within 4.5 blocks of wherever the bot happened to be, in slot order.
  With no visible enemy the goal was `patrolDestination` or spawn 0, so after the opening the bot idled on the spawn island.
  `SkywarsGame.botObjective` returned null on a failed step and nothing else gave it an errand.
- Now: `SkywarsTactics` (pure). Melee goes through the shared `pvpCombat` (strafe, jump, sprint, miss swings, eat, pearl
  at range) with `SkywarsTactics.strafeSafe` (the `SkywarsBotTerrain.safe` footprint rule Bedwars uses), so strafing never
  leaves an island. Chest transfer is ordered sword, armour, blocks, bow, arrows, pearls, apples, food. With nothing in view
  the bot (`SkywarsGame.errand`) walks to the nearest unlooted chest within 40 blocks (scan of the nearby chunks every 5 s,
  gives up on a chest after 20 s), then heads for the best-kitted player within 64 blocks (distance minus 3 per gear point;
  armour and held weapon are public), using the existing bridge code, else paces its island. Hurt (35% or less) with an
  enemy within 5 blocks, Normal and Hard throw a real pearl at their own spawn island (10 s apart). Bow at range was already there.

### Villager Defense and ReadyPlayerZ (co-op)
- Before: target = nearest visible mob; every bot's guard point was the same Mayor location (VD: all bots stack on one
  block) or the exact location of the nearest human (RPZ: standing on top of the player); melee stopped dead at 2.3
  blocks; never backed off; chased mobs anywhere. RPZ already had shop, door buying and revive in `botTick`; VD has no shop or downed state.
- Now: `CoopTactics` (pure). Target = the mob nearest the objective (distance to guard plus 0.6 of own distance, sticky).
  Each bot holds its own lane on an arc round the Mayor (VD, radius 3.5) or beside its human (RPZ, radius 4.5), facing the
  nearest mob, pushed at least 2.5 blocks clear of any human. Co-op melee (`BotService.coopCombat`) strafes in melee, misses
  still swing, backs off when hurt (health at or below max of 35% and retreat-health plus 15%) and holds the lane rather than
  chasing mobs more than 14 blocks from the objective.

### Prop Hunt (classic, infection)
- Before: hunters patrolled map points or picked a random cell within 6 blocks every 5 s, no memory (same corner again and
  again); only the 30 s heartbeat gave them a cue. Props picked a random `bot-hide-points` entry or a cell within 6 blocks,
  locked once and then never moved, however close a hunter got.
- Now: `PropHuntTactics` (pure). Hunters keep a `SearchLedger` (4-block cells, remembered 90 s), mark the cell they stand
  in and the one 3 blocks ahead, and choose among patrol points plus up to 14 random clear cells within 14 blocks, unchecked
  and near first. A heartbeat or a human prop's chicken taunt sends hunters within 30 blocks to that part of the map (10 s memory).
  Props choose from hide points plus nearby cells, far from the hunters' start and apart from the other props. A locked
  prop watches the nearest hunter once a second: seen within 3.5 blocks it may panic and bolt (Easy 50%, Normal 25%, Hard 5%);
  unseen within 6 blocks it may slip away (Easy 10%, Normal 30%, Hard 50%); it runs 8 to 14 blocks away, sprints if panicking, and re-locks on arrival.

### Block Party (classic, hardcore)
- Before: the moment STAND began every bot walked the shortest path to the nearest called tile: no reaction time, never wrong, all identical.
- Now: `BlockPartyTactics` (pure). One decision per bot per call: reaction delay (Easy 14, Normal 8, Hard 3 ticks plus 0 to 5;
  halved in hardcore; capped at a quarter of the call and 20 ticks, inside the call's own walking allowance), and a wrong
  guess (Easy 12%, hardcore 20%; Normal 2%; Hard 0) heading for the nearest other colour. Half of the wrong guesses are
  corrected half way through the call, the rest commit. Hardcore uses the game's own faster timer unchanged.

### TNT Run
- Before: three-tile straight corridors in four directions scored only by length minus a revisit penalty; no sense of how much floor is left.
- Now: `TntRunTactics` (pure) plus `GroundNavigation.nextStepDeep`: score = share of intact floor in the 5 x 5 round the
  tile, minus crossed tiles, plus open neighbours; the first step is then run on in a straight line (3 tiles, 2 on Easy) while safe.
  Easy bots take a random open direction 8% of the time. The old corridor logic remains as the fallback when the deep search finds nothing.

### Dodgeball (solo, teams)
- Before: in `BotService`, face the eye, throw at under 22 blocks, walk at the target beyond 9, back off under 4, otherwise stand
  and trade. No lead, no aim error, no dodge, ignored team numbers.
- Now: `DodgeballTactics` (pure) and `BotService.dodgeballCombat`. Throws lead a moving target (velocity sampled from positions,
  lead capped at 4 blocks, drop compensated) with the tier aim error. A ball whose closest approach is within 1.3 blocks within 14
  ticks triggers a 3-block sidestep (Easy reacts 35% of the time, Normal 70%, Hard 95%; at most one roll per 10 ticks). Hold range
  is 14 blocks when outnumbered, 8 when ahead, 10 level, strafing sideways in between.

### Quake
- Before: decent (strafe, retreat, abilities). Aim error was a yaw-only miss spread of (100 - accuracy) * 0.4 degrees; strafe
  side was a fixed 40-tick toggle, the same rhythm for every bot.
- Now: `QuakeTactics` (pure). Yaw error = tier `aimErrorDegrees` plus 0.15 of the miss chance (Easy about 14, Normal 6, Hard 1.3 degrees),
  pitch error half of that. Strafe flips every 15 to 34 ticks on a per-bot rhythm, circling wide with the tier's `strafeBias`, otherwise a short shuffle (never still).
  There are no pick-up power-ups in Quake; its abilities (shield, sprint, dash, jump) were already used.

### Avalanche and Volcano
- Before: the 3-deep, 32-visit search; hazards only counted when the bot had line of sight to them. Avalanche scored only
  falling-block distance squared. Volcano multiplied height by a flat 12 whatever the lava was doing and ignored the smoke-column rock.
- Now: both use `nextStepDeep` (6 deep, 96 visits) and `HazardBotTactics` (pure, shared). Falling blocks are public and
  telegraphed, so no line of sight is needed; danger is weighted by time to land. Avalanche adds an open-space bonus to stay out of corners.
  Volcano climbs harder as the lava gets closer (30, 18 or 10 points a block), treats cells at or just above the lava as poison, treats the next rise as a hazard in its last second, and avoids the pending rock column.

## Shared files touched (all additive)
- `BotService`: a co-op target re-pick hook after the enemy is chosen (`coopTarget`), a `skywarsMelee` branch, a `coopCombat` branch, the dodgeball body replaced by `dodgeballCombat`, and the Skywars objective call passing `engaged`. The player-target selection lines (Bedwars decoys) are untouched.
- `SurfaceMemory.nextStepDeep` (new overload), `DodgeballGame.liveBalls()`, `SkywarsGame.botObjective` 5-argument overload (the 4-argument one still behaves as before).
- Tests: `BotServiceTest.activeCoop` gives the bot health 20, a zombie name and a walkable seam (the new co-op melee strafes and reads health); `ZDifficultyBotSummaryTest` now asserts a bot stands within 5 blocks of its nearest human rather than on the exact spot.

## Config keys
None. Uses `BotDifficulty` profile values (`aimErrorDegrees`, `strafeBias`, `retreatHealth`, `hitChance`) so `bots.combat.tiers.*` overrides apply.

## Left for later
- Skywars: no tower-up, water-bucket clutch or TNT/lava-bucket use (insane loot); bots do not take cover from arrows; chest scan assumes tile entities are loaded within 2 chunks.
- Co-op: VD has no shop or downed state to use; RPZ perks and the mystery box are not bought by bots; no priority for special mobs; lanes do not react to doors opening mid-wave.
- Prop Hunt: hunters do not use the Pulse power on a schedule beyond the existing rule; props still re-lock only through the normal lock rules (a refused lock sends them to pick a new spot).
- Block Party: bots do not avoid crowding on the target patch; the wrong-colour choice is the nearest other tile, not a deliberate decoy.
- TNT Run: doublejump mode bots never double-jump; no sense of other bots' lines. Dodgeball: no catching or ball pick-up exists in the game (infinite blaze-rod throws), so none was added; dodging is limited by the 4 Hz think and Citizens walking speed. Quake: no pick-ups to use.
- Avalanche: shrinking edge is read from the next area only; Volcano: no use of blocks to bridge gaps.

## In-game acceptance (Java and Bedrock; bots are server-side, so both see the same behaviour)
1. Skywars solo, 3 bots: within a minute each loots its own island, then walks or bridges towards another player; none stands on spawn after the opening. Melee strafes without walking off the island edge. Hurt bots sometimes pearl home.
2. Skywars insane: bots take swords and armour first (check what they wear and hold after the first chest).
3. Villager Defense: bots spread round the Mayor, not stacked; they hit the zombie nearest the Mayor first; a low-health bot backs off.
4. ReadyPlayerZ: bots stand beside, not on, the human; still buy doors and revive a downed human.
5. Prop Hunt: hunters sweep different rooms, go to a taunt (chicken) within 30 blocks; a hunter walking up to a bot prop sometimes makes it run (more on Easy).
6. Block Party Easy: bots hesitate about a second and a few walk to the wrong colour; Hard bots move almost immediately; hardcore gives them less time.
7. TNT Run: bots keep running towards the part of the floor that is still intact; none stands still.
8. Dodgeball: a bot visibly leads a running player and sidesteps incoming throws; outnumbered bots keep their distance.
9. Quake: Easy bots miss visibly more than Hard; bots change strafe side at different moments.
10. Avalanche: bots step out from under landing markers and stay out of corners. Volcano: bots climb faster as lava gets near and dodge the smoke-column rock.
