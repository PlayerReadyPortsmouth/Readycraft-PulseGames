A wardrobe worth earning

Cosmetics, progression and trophies that travel with the player.

One wardrobe, across ReadyCraft

Let players earn something in a minigame, wear it in the lobby and display the achievement at home in SurvivalTest. PulseContent should own the cosmetic catalogue, presentation and shared wardrobe API. PulseGames, PulseAddon and future Creative competitions should award recognised rewards through that API.

These additions are not implemented or deployed. Initial network scope: Lobby, SurvivalTest, MinigamesTest and the primary proxy. Older servers remain separate.

Practice and bot-filled games need an explicit published reward policy. Proposed: allow capped participation progress, but reserve competitive rankings and scarce championship awards for eligible human matches. Preserve existing restrictions until this policy is agreed.

Fairness and comfort are part of the catalogue

Each cosmetic carries context rules: lobby, Survival, waiting room, active match, spectator and celebration. During Prop Hunt, suppress hider accessories, followers, trails, sounds and glows that reveal a disguise. During PvP, preserve armour readability, hitboxes, team colours and projectile recognition. Cosmetic ownership must never alter damage, reach, scale, collision or visibility rules.

Provide separate controls for own effects, other players’ effects, sounds and animation intensity, with a quiet preset. Use particle budgets, distance culling and companion limits. Text and icons must communicate rarity and status without relying on colour. Support Java inventory menus and Bedrock-friendly forms or an equivalent tested menu flow; do not assume a Java preview proves Bedrock parity.

Primary proxyTransport and network context; not an authority that invents reward ownership

Use separate SQL records for entitlements, award receipts, equipped slots and trophy claims. A unique player + source-event + reward key makes retries safe. Token purchase must debit and grant atomically. Persist the award intent before retrying an unavailable database; never tell a player it succeeded before acknowledgement. Cross-server cache invalidation needs a bounded refresh fallback.

Identify accounts by trusted UUIDs and explicit Java/Floodgate account linking, never by display name. Record a versioned legacy-ID mapping and migrate existing unlocks without deleting originals. The current PulseAddon SQL document store is a backend migration foundation, not by itself a concurrent network wardrobe.

Trophies that survive server moves

An event award records its owner, event, season, category, placing and grant reference. The owner can redeem a display in a permitted Survival claim. Placement receives its own unique ID; breaking, moving, rollback and reclaim must reconcile that ID so a crash cannot duplicate a valuable display. Reclaim retires or invalidates the previous placement before issuing another usable display.

Use PulseContent models with a readable fallback. Show the event and earned distinction on inspection. Keep owner-bound trophies distinct from ordinary tradable decorations. Staff can revoke a mistaken award with an audit record rather than editing inventory tags. Map-art redemption can reuse the claim and display plumbing later, but artwork approval remains a separate review workflow.

PulseContent currently caps an input catalogue at 256 items and 32 MiB total item textures in the inspected parser; large collections need measured pack budgets and deliberate expansion, not unlimited assets. Its initial item bases are paper, amethyst shard and flint. Do not promise hats, back attachments or followers from item creation alone.

Start with the shared foundation and a small collection that demonstrates variety. Expand based on what players actually equip, while maintaining inexpensive, quiet choices alongside elaborate rewards. Artwork, model creation and in-game acceptance remain separate work to be scheduled.

User-created content boundary

Built-in cosmetics use reviewed catalogue assets. For future map art and competition content, record the user’s no-image-import requirement. Scan rendered player-created artwork to assist moderation, then route it to human review; automated classification is an aid, not approval. Keep public exhibition and Survival redemption blocked until a reviewer approves the exact revision. Any edit invalidates that approval. Include reporting, reasons, appeal and removal of already-issued displays where required. The creative workflow should define review access and retention before collecting submission images.
